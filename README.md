## Why Reproducibility matters in social research?
In social research, reproducibility ensures academic integrity and safeguards public decision-making. Because social phenomena involve complex human behaviors and flexible statistical modeling, studies are vulnerable to p-hacking and subjective data manipulation. Requiring full transparency of raw datasets, survey instruments, and analysis scripts allows peers to audit methodological choices independently. Moreover, governments rely heavily on sociological and economic findings to design welfare systems, educational reforms, and tax policies. An unverified error can lead to harmful legislation. Reproducibility separates robust social patterns from statistical artifacts, preserving the field's scientific validity and rebuilding public trust.
